MSP3520 video player v5 (column-interlace A/B + 80 MHz experiment build)
=======================================================================

WHAT'S IN THIS FOLDER
  bootloader.bin                flash to 0x0
  partition-table.bin           flash to 0x8000
  msp3520_video_oc_test.bin     flash to 0x10000  (app + embedded video)
  msp3520_video_oc_test_full.bin  all three merged - flash to 0x0

FLASH (pick one; wiring is unchanged from the earlier builds:
SCK=12 MOSI=11 MISO=13 CS=10 DC=9 RESET=14 LED=47)
  idf.py -p COMx flash            (from the msp3520-video-oc-test project)
  or: python -m esptool --chip esp32s3 -b 460800 write-flash 0x0 \
        bootloader.bin 0x8000 partition-table.bin 0x10000 \
        msp3520_video_oc_test.bin
  or: python -m esptool --chip esp32s3 -b 460800 write-flash 0x0 \
        msp3520_video_oc_test_full.bin

WHAT IT DOES
  1. Boots, reads the controller ID, fills the screen dark blue.
  2. Sweeps the same 119-frame video at 10..80 MHz in 5 MHz steps,
     10 s per step. Press keys DURING playback (single keypress,
     no Enter): y = clean, n = artifacts, r = restart, q = quit.
  3. Prints a summary table, then a retest loop (type MHz + Enter),
     then a continuous-playback mode (type MHz + Enter).

KEYS DURING PLAYBACK
  y = clean    n = artifacts   r = restart video   q = quit sweep
  d = cycle SCK/MOSI drive capability 0..3 (80 MHz experiment)
  i = TOGGLE COLUMN-INTERLACE on/off (new in v5, below)

THE 'i' EXPERIMENT - WHAT IT CAN AND CANNOT DO
  Default (progressive): every frame is one full-screen update,
  ~10.5 fps at 40 MHz - the RGB666 wire ceiling for this panel.
  Press i: each frame is sent as TWO half-screen updates - the left
  240-px half, then the right half ~46 ms later. The update rate
  doubles to ~21 per second; the status line shows both numbers,
  e.g. "10.3 fps (20.6 upd/s)".

  HONEST PHYSICS (research-verified, see WORKLOG sections 12/14):
  the wire still carries every byte of every frame, so the frame
  rate ceiling does NOT change, and no pixel updates faster than
  ~10.85 Hz. Interlacing halves the bytes per FIELD, never the
  bytes per PICTURE - it cannot create frames the source does not
  have. What may improve is perceived cadence (regular ~46 ms
  updates instead of irregular full-frame steps); whether that
  looks smoother is content-dependent and a matter of taste.
  Judge with your own eyes: play the same MHz step with i off,
  then on.

  Expected artifacts in interlaced mode: a stationary vertical seam
  at mid-screen; during fast horizontal pans the two sides can be
  ~46 ms of motion apart (shear at the seam). Static content:
  identical to progressive.

  Requires PSRAM (the frame stage lives there - ESP32-S3 N8R2 or
  N8R8 class). Without PSRAM the firmware says so and stays
  progressive.

THE 80 MHz EXPERIMENT (unchanged from v4.1)
  The 65-80 MHz steps all run at 80 MHz actual (the S3 has no SPI
  clock between 40 and 80). If they show snow:
    1. press d repeatedly while watching - weaker SCK/MOSI edges
       (settings 0/1) ring less on jumper wires
    2. 47-100 ohm series resistor in the SCK line at the ESP32 end
    3. a GND jumper routed alongside SCK; keep wires under 10 cm
  Research verdict: LOW-MEDIUM odds (the ILI9488 die is 4x out of
  spec at 80 MHz; 40 MHz is the highest reliably reported SPI clock
  for this controller anywhere) - but this is the only remaining
  $0 path to 15 fps on this module, so it is worth the attempt.

EXPECTED NUMBERS (40 MHz actual = the 35..60 MHz steps)
  progressive : ~10.5 fps
  interlaced  : ~10.2-10.5 fps, ~20.5-21 upd/s (the stage decode is
                not overlapped, costing a few percent vs theory)
  10 MHz      : ~2.7 fps / ~5.4 upd/s (plenty for judging artifacts)

If interlaced mode looks WORSE, just leave it off - progressive is
the default and nothing else changed.
